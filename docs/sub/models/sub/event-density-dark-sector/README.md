# Event-Density Dark Sector Conjecture

Status: open formalization task

The Event-Density Dark Sector Conjecture is a speculative Ontoverse hypothesis about whether some dark-matter-like gravitational effects could correspond to an effective contribution from undetected **causal-processing-density** structures and their influence on branch-local transition rates.

This page does not claim that dark matter has been explained. It defines a cautious conceptual bridge between causal processing density, effective transition rate, temporal density, local time-rate gradients, metric deviation, and dark-matter-like observations.

## Terminology Correction

This conjecture previously risked conflating **temporal density** with **causal processing density**.

They are now treated as distinct quantities:

```text
temporal density
= significant event-nodes / frontal-time interval

causal processing density
= local state-coordination or processing load relative to frontal-time slices
```

Under the current Ontoverse interpretation, higher temporal density means more significant branch-local transitions over the same `ΔF` and therefore more local-time accumulation.

The gravity conjecture instead concerns a possible increase in **causal processing load** that could reduce the effective rate of significant transitions.

## Core Formulation

Dark-matter-like gravitational effects may correspond to an effective metric contribution from undetected causal-processing-density structures.

The current speculative chain is:

```text
higher causal processing density
-> lower effective significant-transition rate
-> lower temporal density
-> slower local-time accumulation relative to frontal time
-> spatial local time-rate gradient
-> effective metric deviation
-> gravitational or dark-matter-like effect
```

Every arrow in this chain beyond the definitions is speculative and requires formalization.

## Motivation

Many dark matter searches assume that dark matter can be detected through rare interactions with ordinary matter, such as particle-like scattering, recoil, ionization, scintillation, or other localized detector signals.

Ontoverse allows a different conceptual question:

```text
What if some dark-matter-like effects are associated not with a directly detectable particle-like substance,
but with an effective metric contribution from hidden local causal-processing structure?
```

This does not reject particle dark matter. It only defines a speculative alternative interpretation that must be compared against particle-based dark matter, modified gravity, and existing cosmological constraints.

## Relation to Frontal-Time Slices

The current Frontal Time Model treats each frontal-time value `F` as a global history-space slice `S(F)`.

A branch may accumulate significant event-nodes more or less frequently across the same sequence of slices.

The conjecture therefore distinguishes:

```text
shared frontal-time progression
-> S(F0), S(F1), S(F2), ...

branch-local processing load
-> may differ by region

branch-local transition frequency
-> may differ as a consequence
```

A high local causal-processing load is conjectured to reduce the effective rate at which significant transitions accumulate relative to the same global frontal-time progression.

## Relation to Gravity

In this conjecture, causal processing density does not gravitate as a particle-like object.

A more careful formulation is:

```text
The effective metric deformation associated with local causal-processing structure is what may appear gravitational.
```

Therefore, the bridge to gravity is not:

```text
temporal density = mass
```

and not:

```text
more event-nodes = stronger gravity
```

The current conjectural chain is instead:

```text
causal processing load distribution
-> effective transition-rate distribution
-> local time-rate distribution
-> local time-rate gradient
-> metric deviation
-> gravitational effect
```

This keeps the conjecture aligned with the broader Ontoverse distinction between global frontal-time ordering and branch-local time accumulation.

## Relation to Existing Ontoverse Terms

This conjecture depends on several existing Ontoverse terms:

- **Frontal-Time Slice** — a global history-space cross-section at one frontal-time value.
- **Event-Node** — a significant transition point in a historical trajectory.
- **Temporal Density** — the frequency of significant event-nodes per frontal-time interval.
- **Causal Processing Density** — the proposed amount of local state coordination or processing load relative to frontal-time slices.
- **Effective Quantum Transition Rate** — a placeholder for the effective rate of significant branch-local transitions relative to frontal time.
- **Local Time** — time experienced along a particular historical trajectory.
- **Local Time-Rate Gradient** — spatial difference in local time rate across neighboring regions.
- **Local Metric Deviation** — local departure from a branch baseline metric.

The conjecture suggests that a persistent causal-processing structure could contribute to local time-rate gradients and metric deviations even if it does not produce an ordinary electromagnetic detector signal.

## Dark-Matter-Like Interpretation

In standard observational language, dark matter is inferred mainly from gravitational effects rather than direct electromagnetic visibility.

Ontoverse does not currently claim to identify the physical substance or mechanism behind those effects.

It only proposes the following open possibility:

```text
Some dark-matter-like gravitational effects may be interpretable as effective metric contributions
from undetected causal-processing-density structures.
```

If such structures do not interact with ordinary matter through typical electromagnetic or particle-scattering channels, then detectors designed to observe particle-like recoil or energy deposition may fail to detect them directly.

This is not evidence for the conjecture. It is only an explanation of why the conjecture would not necessarily produce a standard direct-detection signal.

## Required Tests and Constraints

For this conjecture to become more than an analogy, it must explain or reproduce at least the following:

- galaxy rotation curves;
- gravitational lensing profiles;
- galaxy cluster behavior;
- structure formation;
- cosmic microwave background constraints;
- the absence or weakness of direct-detection signals;
- compatibility with known relativistic and quantum constraints;
- whether the model predicts anything distinct from particle dark matter or modified gravity.

## Open Formalization Tasks

The main task is to define a mathematical bridge between causal processing density and metric deviation.

A possible symbolic sketch is:

```text
rho_process(x, F)
-> Gamma_eff(x, F)
-> d_tau/dF(x)
-> metric deviation
```

Where:

- `rho_process(x, F)` represents local causal-processing density at frontal-time slice `F`;
- `Gamma_eff(x, F)` represents the effective significant-transition rate;
- `tau` represents local time;
- `d_tau/dF` represents local time accumulation relative to frontal time;
- spatial variation of `d_tau/dF` may correspond to a local time-rate gradient;
- that gradient would need to map to an effective metric or gravitational contribution.

None of these mappings are currently defined rigorously.

## Boundaries

This conjecture does not currently claim to:

- explain dark matter;
- disprove particle dark matter;
- replace general relativity;
- replace quantum mechanics;
- provide a tested equation;
- predict galaxy rotation curves;
- reproduce gravitational lensing;
- satisfy cosmological constraints;
- show that causal processing density is a physical substance;
- show that temporal density itself directly causes gravity.

It is a speculative Ontoverse model component and an open formalization task.

## Short Safe Summary

Some dark-matter-like effects may correspond to an effective gravitational contribution from undetected causal-processing-density structures. In the current Ontoverse interpretation, such structures could hypothetically reduce local significant-transition rates relative to frontal time, producing local time-rate gradients and an effective metric contribution. This remains an unformalized conjecture.
